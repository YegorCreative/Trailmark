# Sequoia sources

Live pages used:

- Fact sheet (shared with Kings Canyon): https://www.nps.gov/seki/learn/news/fact-sheet.htm
  - Sequoia NP established September 25, 1890. Elevation range 1,370 feet (Middle Fork Kaweah River) to 14,494 feet (Mount Whitney), the highest point in the contiguous US. General Sherman Tree: 274.9 feet tall, 52,508 cubic feet volume, about 2,200 years old. Giant Forest (Sequoia) 2,312 acres. 321 wildlife species (300 native) across the joint parks. Combined acreage 865,964; wilderness 808,078 (about 93.3%). Sequoia's own acreage 404,064 (from an earlier WebSearch summary of the same NPS data).
- History and culture index: https://www.nps.gov/seki/learn/historyculture/index.htm
  - "On September 25th, 1890, President Benjamin Harrison signed legislation establishing the second national park in America — Sequoia National Park," created specifically to protect giant sequoias from logging; described as the first park formed specifically to protect a living organism.
- Moro Rock: https://www.nps.gov/thingstodo/moro-rock.htm
  - Ascend 300 feet over 0.25 miles; over 350 steps on a concrete and stone stairway. Views include the foothills and San Joaquin Valley to the west, the Great Western Divide to the east, and on clear days California's Coast Range.
- Crystal Cave: https://www.nps.gov/thingstodo/crystal-cave.htm
  - Marble cave formed by an underground creek. Tickets sold only online through Sequoia Parks Conservancy, not at the park. Tours spring through fall only, about 50 minutes, preceded by a steep half-mile walk descending 300 feet.
- WebSearch summary of NPS results re: John Muir: Muir stayed several days in 1875 in a hollowed sequoia "house in a log," calling it his "noble den" in his book Our National Parks.

No page opened for this essay states a specific age or exact wingspan-equivalent figure that isn't already listed above; General Sherman's status as largest "by volume," not tallest or oldest, is stated explicitly per the fact sheet's own framing and is preserved as such in the essay.
