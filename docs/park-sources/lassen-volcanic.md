# Lassen Volcanic sources

Live pages used:

- Press kit: https://www.nps.gov/lavo/learn/news/presskit.htm
  - Total acreage 106,505. Wilderness 79,062 acres (about 74%), designated October 19, 1972. 172 miles of trail; 40 miles paved road, 23 miles unpaved. National forest designation June 2, 1905; Lassen Peak and Cinder Cone became national monuments May 6, 1907; Lassen Volcanic National Park established August 9, 1916. Most recent eruption May 19, 1915. 2023 visitation 418,978; 10-year average 515,545. 300 vertebrate species; 805 confirmed vascular plant species.
- Volcanoes page: https://www.nps.gov/lavo/learn/nature/volcanoes.htm
  - "Represented in the park are all four types of volcanoes found in the world — shield, composite, cinder cone, and plug dome." Recent eruptions from Cinder Cone (about 350 years ago) and Lassen Peak (about 100 years ago, i.e. 1915).
- Hike Lassen Peak: https://www.nps.gov/thingstodo/hikelassenpeak.htm
  - Trailhead 8,500 feet; elevation gain 1,957 feet; round trip 5 miles; rated strenuous. Described as "one of the largest plug dome volcanoes in the world."
- Hike Bumpass Hell: https://www.nps.gov/thingstodo/hikebumpasshell.htm
  - 3-mile round trip, about 90 minutes. Trail descends 300 feet into the basin; boardwalk provides access. Called "the largest hydrothermal area in the park." Ground can hide acidic boiling water or mud beneath a solid-looking crust; visitors have been severely injured off-trail.
- WebSearch summary of NPS results: Lassen volcanic center hosts "the most extensive hydrothermal system in the Cascade arc."

Lassen Peak's elevation (10,457 feet) is not stated as a single number on any page opened for this essay — it is only derivable by adding the Lassen Peak trailhead elevation (8,500 ft) and gain (1,957 ft) from the hiking page. The essay does not state a summit elevation as a stand-alone confirmed figure for that reason.
