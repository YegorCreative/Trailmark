# Crater Lake sources

Live pages opened:

- History and culture, last updated February 3, 2023: https://www.nps.gov/crla/learn/historyculture/index.htm
  - Founded May 22, 1902.
  - Hillman party, June 12, 1853. Skeeters suggested Deep Blue Lake. Chauncy Nye, 1862, first published description, Blue Lake. William Gladstone Steel read about the lake in 1870, saw it in 1885, and then spent seventeen years working to make it a park.
  - Sandals under ash about 7,700 years old. Makalaks (Klamath) silence about the lake.
- The lake, last updated September 8, 2023: https://www.nps.gov/crla/learn/nature/crater-lake.htm
  - Eruption about 7,700 years ago. Caldera 5–6 miles across and 0.7 miles deep. Mount Mazama about 12,000 feet before collapse.
  - Hillman Peak 8,151 feet. Mount Scott 8,929 feet. Wizard Island. Last eruption on its east flank about 4,800 years ago.
  - Average depth 1,943 feet. About 4.9 trillion gallons. About 34 billion gallons gained and lost each year.
  - Clarity averages 102 feet. Deepest lake in the United States. Top ten in the world. Rarely freezes.
  - Snowfall as of 2020: average 42 feet a year, 51 feet in the 1930s. Precipitation 67 inches.
  - Mazama newts endemic. Seven fish species introduced between 1886 and 1941. Two survived: rainbow trout and the salmon spelled Kokonee on the page. Crayfish introduced in 1915.
  - Moss between 85 and 460 feet. Deep Rover, 1987–1989, 47 dives. Old Man of the Lake: 30-foot hemlock, three feet above water, tracked by Joseph Diller in 1896, carbon dated beyond 450 years. Diller's phrase is quoted in the essay.
- Nature index, last updated May 9, 2022: https://www.nps.gov/crla/learn/nature/index.htm
  - Over 700 native plants. About 16 wildfires a year, most lightning.
