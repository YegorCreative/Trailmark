# Kings Canyon sources

Live pages used:

- Fact sheet: https://www.nps.gov/seki/learn/news/fact-sheet.htm
  - Sequoia NP established September 25, 1890. Kings Canyon NP established March 4, 1940 (General Grant NP established October 1, 1890, later abolished into Kings Canyon). Elevation range 1,370 feet (Middle Fork Kaweah, Sequoia) to 14,494 feet (Mount Whitney, Sequoia). General Sherman Tree (Sequoia) 274.9 feet, 52,508 cubic feet, about 2,200 years old. Redwood Mountain Grove (Kings Canyon) 2,558 acres; Giant Forest (Sequoia) 2,312 acres. 321 wildlife species (300 native). 275 caves across the joint parks.
- History and culture index: https://www.nps.gov/seki/learn/historyculture/index.htm
  - Sequoia NP signed by President Benjamin Harrison September 25, 1890, to protect giant sequoias from logging. General Grant NP established one week later, four square miles. Kings Canyon NP created 1940 by Congress and President Franklin D. Roosevelt, absorbing General Grant NP; jointly administered since. Homelands of the Mono (Monache), Yokuts, Tübatulabal, Paiute, and Western Shoshone.
- Kings Canyon National Park visitor page: https://www.nps.gov/seki/planyourvisit/kings-canyon-national-park.htm
  - Two districts: Grant Grove (General Grant Tree, North Grove Trail, Big Stump, Panoramic Point) and Cedar Grove (Roaring River Falls, Muir Rock, Knapp's Cabin, Zumwalt Meadow). Access via Highway 180 (Big Stump) or Highway 198 (Ash Mountain). Cedar Grove closes seasonally for snow.
- General Grant Tree facts: https://www.nps.gov/places/000/general-grant-tree.htm
  - Height 268.1 feet. Ground circumference 107.5 feet. Trunk volume 46,608 cubic feet. Second-largest tree in the world by volume. Holds the titles Nation's Christmas Tree and the country's only living national shrine; the page does not give a founding year for either title, so none is stated in the essay as confirmed on this page.
- Combined acreage from search summary of NPS results: 865,964 acres total (Sequoia 404,064; Kings Canyon 461,901); wilderness 808,078 acres (about 93.3%).

No statistics page gives a Kings Canyon depth figure; third-party claims (7,700-8,200 feet, deeper than the Grand Canyon) are not from nps.gov and were left out.
