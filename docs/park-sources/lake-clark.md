# Lake Clark sources

Live pages used:

- Park statistics: https://www.nps.gov/lacl/learn/management/statistics.htm
  - Total 4,030,006 acres: 2,619,713 acres national park, 1,410,293 acres national preserve. 2.61 million acres added to the National Wilderness Preservation System in 1980. Designated a national monument December 1, 1978; national park and preserve December 2, 1980, by ANILCA. Mount Redoubt 10,197 feet (highest point in park), Mount Iliamna 10,016 feet, both active volcanoes on the Pacific Ring of Fire. Redoubt eruptions recorded in 1902, 1966, 1989, and 2009. Kvichak watershed sockeye run 147,000 to 3.1 million fish annually; about 13,000 red salmon harvested by subsistence users upstream of the Newhalen counting station. Lake Clark elevation 254 feet, maximum depth 870 feet.
- Park homepage: https://www.nps.gov/lacl/index.htm
  - "Volcanoes steam, salmon run, bears forage, and craggy mountains reflect in shimmering turquoise lakes." Ancestral Dena'ina homelands. World's largest sockeye salmon fishery named. Lake Clark called the largest lake by volume in the National Park Service. Cook Inlet coast, 130 miles, called unrivaled brown bear habitat. Proenneke National Historic Site named as a symbol of the wilderness movement. Perennial snow and ice cover about 1,250 square miles (20% of the park); boreal forest over 440,000 acres (11%).
- Proenneke's Cabin: https://www.nps.gov/lacl/learn/historyculture/proennekes-cabin.htm
  - Cabin built during the summers of 1967 and 1968 by hand, local materials. Proenneke lived there 30 years without electricity, running water, or telephone. Listed on the National Register of Historic Places in 2007.
