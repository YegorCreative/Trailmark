# Dry Tortugas sources

Live pages used:

- History and culture: https://www.nps.gov/drto/learn/historyculture/index.htm
  - Monument January 4, 1935, 47,125 acres. Expanded 1983. National park October 26, 1992. Seven keys. About 70 miles west of Key West. Fort built 1846–1875. Largest all-masonry fort in the United States.
- A part of American history: https://www.nps.gov/drto/learn/historyculture/dry-tortugas-a-part-of-american-history.htm
  - Enslaved labor from Key West. Obsolete by 1862. Prison, Dr. Mudd, 1867 yellow fever. Army left 1874. Rededicated October 26, 1992. Nearly 2,000 people at the fort's height.
- Fort Jefferson: https://www.nps.gov/drto/learn/historyculture/fort-jefferson.htm
  - 1846–1875, never finished. Civil War prison. USS Maine departed in 1898.
- Enabling legislation: https://www.nps.gov/drto/learn/management/enablinglegislation.htm
  - Monument abolished. Purposes include loggerhead and green turtles, sooty terns, frigate birds, Fort Jefferson, and submerged resources.
- Homepage: https://www.nps.gov/drto/
  - About 100 square miles, mostly water. Seven islands. More than 16 million bricks. More than 300 sunken ships. Boat or seaplane only.
- Places: https://www.nps.gov/drto/learn/historyculture/places.htm
  - Garden Key light 1825. Loggerhead light begun 1856.
- Map and guide PDF notes Ponce de León, 1513, Las Tortugas, and the 1908 sooty tern refuge.

The hatchling drawing is not a nest count. Entrance fees are left to NPS. The history page uses the phrase Gulf of America; the essay follows that page.
