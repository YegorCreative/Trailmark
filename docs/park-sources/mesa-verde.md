# Mesa Verde sources

Live pages opened:

- History and culture, last updated December 31, 2025: https://www.nps.gov/meve/learn/historyculture/index.htm
  - Established June 29, 1906, by Theodore Roosevelt, to preserve cultural heritage, the first national park of its kind.
  - Nearly 5,000 ancestral sites. Cliff Palace is the largest cliff dwelling. Ancestral Pueblo people lived here over 700 years ago.
- Places, last updated April 27, 2026: https://www.nps.gov/meve/learn/historyculture/places.htm
  - Over 81 square miles. Ancestral Pueblo inhabitation about 550–1300 CE.
  - More than 52,000 acres and the most extensive concentration of well-preserved prehistoric cliff dwellings in the United States.
  - More than 5,000 archeological sites, including 600 alcove dwellings.
  - Pithouses about 550 CE. Most cliff dwellings built 1180–1280 CE. Far View on Chapin Mesa. Sun Temple may be unfinished.
  - Alcoves carved by natural springs. Ute, Navajo, and Apache temporary camps. Yucca House is a separate monument with no valley sites open inside Mesa Verde.

The history index says nearly 5,000 sites and the places page says more than 5,000. Both are recorded. The collared lizard is the archive drawing, not a wildlife census. No establishment-year conflict: the park's own history page says June 29, 1906.
