# Kenai Fjords sources

Live pages used:

- 2025 fact sheet (NPS PDF): https://www.nps.gov/kefj/learn/management/upload/KEFJ_Fact-Sheet-2025-508.pdf
  - Total acreage 669,983. Wilderness 649,219. Proclaimed December 2, 1980. Harding Icefield about 700 square miles. Over 30 tidewater and piedmont glaciers. Elevation sea level to 6,471 feet (Mount Marathon area).
- Basic information: https://www.nps.gov/kefj/planyourvisit/basicinfo.htm
  - Over 600,000 acres. Designated a national park in 1980 through ANILCA. Exit Glacier is the road-accessible feature. Described as remote, rugged, and unpredictable.
- Park homepage: https://www.nps.gov/kefj/index.htm
  - Nearly 40 glaciers originate from the Harding Icefield, the park's "crowning feature." Shrinking glaciers named as evidence of climate change. Orcas, humpback whales, mountain goats, seals named.
- KEFJ Story: https://www.nps.gov/kefj/kefj-story.htm
  - Alutiiq (Sugpiaq) village site from the 1790s-1800s Russian-contact period. Ground slate tools, kayaks and umiaq (angyaq) trade vessels, Chinese beads and Alaskan coal as evidence of trade networks. Russian-era iron knives and coins found alongside traditional tools.
- Geodiversity Atlas / WebSearch of nps.gov: proclaimed a national monument December 1, 1978 (Proclamation 4620, President Carter); redesignated a national park December 2, 1980, by ANILCA.

Conflicting figures: the Geodiversity Atlas gives 669,984 acres (converted from 271,132 hectares) against the fact sheet's 669,983. Difference is a one-acre rounding artifact, not a real conflict; the fact sheet figure is used as the more current, dedicated statistics source. An earlier search snippet gave the Harding Icefield as 800 square miles (2,072 km², Geodiversity Atlas); the 2025 fact sheet's "approximately 700 square miles" is used instead as the dedicated statistics page.
