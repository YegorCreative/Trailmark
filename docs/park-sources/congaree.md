# Congaree sources

Live pages opened:

- People in the Floodplain, last updated January 7, 2026: https://www.nps.gov/cong/learn/historyculture/history-culture.htm
  - Congaree Swamp National Monument, 1976. Redesignated a national park in 2003. Over 26,000 acres.
  - Largest remaining tract of southern old-growth bottomland forest. Named for the Congaree people.
  - At least 10,000 years of habitation. De Soto, April 1541. John Lawson, 1701. Maroons. Francis Beidler, 15,000 acres, logging ceased by 1917. Harry Hampton. Congaree Action Now.
  - The day of the 2003 redesignation is not on this page. It was not copied from the foundation PDF.
- History index, last updated August 4, 2016: https://www.nps.gov/cong/learn/historyculture/index.htm
  - People using the floodplain for over 13,000 years. That figure is kept separate from the 10,000-year sentence.
- Nature, last updated October 6, 2024: https://www.nps.gov/cong/learn/nature/index.htm
  - Flood and fire. Bald cypress and tupelo. Oaks and loblolly. Longleaf remnants.
  - Red-cockaded woodpecker, indigo snake, southern fox squirrel threatened or endangered.
  - Former extent: about 30 million acres of bottomland, about 90 million acres of longleaf savannah.
- Wilderness: https://www.nps.gov/cong/congaree-wilderness.htm
  - 21,700 acres designated wilderness. Entire park 26,692.6 acres.
- FAQ, last updated July 19, 2023: https://www.nps.gov/cong/faqs.htm
  - 2.6-mile boardwalk. Canopy average over 100 feet. Champion loblolly last measured 170 feet, circumference over 15 feet.
  - No entrance fee. Bikes only on the entrance road. Dogs on a leash no more than 6 feet. Fishing needs a South Carolina license. No canoe rental.
- Management, last updated February 10, 2026: https://www.nps.gov/cong/learn/management/index.htm
  - Authorized by Congress in 1976. Points to the foundation document for the establishment story.

Fireflies in the extra drawing are not a species count. Current water levels are left to NPS.
