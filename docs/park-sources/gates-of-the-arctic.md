# Gates of the Arctic sources

Live pages used:

- What is Gates of the Arctic: https://www.nps.gov/gaar/learn/what-is-gates-of-the-arctic.htm
  - Established December 2, 1980, ANILCA. 8.4 million acres. Sport hunting in the preserve, not the park. Wilderness about 7,052,000 acres, later about 7,154,000. Six wild and scenic rivers, including the Alatna, John, and Kobuk. About five miles a day on foot.
- Creation: https://www.nps.gov/gaar/learn/historyculture/creation-of-gates-of-the-arctic-national-park-and-preserve.htm
  - Carter monuments in 1978. 1980 act. About eight million contiguous acres. Not the small park split by a pipeline.
- The gates: https://www.nps.gov/gaar/learn/historyculture/the-gates-of-the-arctic.htm
  - Robert Marshall, 1929, North Fork of the Koyukuk. Frigid Crags and Boreal Mountain.
- History index: https://www.nps.gov/gaar/learn/historyculture/index.htm
  - More than 13,000 years. Eleven resident-zone communities.
- Basic information: https://www.nps.gov/gaar/planyourvisit/basicinfo.htm
  - 8.4 million acres. No implication of a road. A 12,000-year record. "You're on your own."
- Hours: https://www.nps.gov/gaar/planyourvisit/hours.htm
  - No roads or marked trails. Planes and boats in summer. Skis and snowmobiles in enough snow.
- Anaktuvuk Pass: https://www.nps.gov/gaar/historyculture/anaktuvuk-pass.htm
  - Village as an inholding. ATV conflict after 1980.
- Homepage: https://www.nps.gov/gaar/
  - No roads or trails. Caribou migrate. People for over ten thousand years.

The three caribou are a drawing, not a herd count. The other three wild rivers were not named from a complete list in the extract used here.
