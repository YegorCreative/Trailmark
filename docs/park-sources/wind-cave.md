# Wind Cave sources

Live pages used:

- WebSearch summary of NPS results: established January 9, 1903, the first cave anywhere designated a national park. About 95% of the world's discovered boxwork is found in Wind Cave. Wind Cave is one of the longest, oldest, and most complex caves on Earth, and the densest cave system in the world by passage volume per cubic mile. Park acreage about 33,924 acres (13,728 hectares).
- Boxwork: https://www.nps.gov/wica/learn/nature/speleothems-boxwork.htm
  - Boxwork is thin calcite blades forming a honeycomb pattern on cave walls/ceilings. Formed along fractures from gypsum drying/rehydrating cycles; calcite filled fractures, softer surrounding bedrock weathered away, exposing the boxwork. "Perhaps in no other cave in the world is boxwork so well-formed and abundant as in Wind Cave." Formation origin still called "one of the biggest mysteries of Wind Cave."
- WebSearch summary re: Lakota origin/barometric wind: Lakota oral tradition names a passageway to the spirit lodge, Oniya Oshoka, where the earth "breathes"; modern Lakota name Maka Oniye, "breathing earth." First humans and bison said to have emerged from the spirit lodge through this entrance. Wind caused by barometric pressure differences between cave and surface, equalizing with day/night and weather changes. At least 20 different native nations have ties to Wind Cave.
- Wind Cave's Natural Entrance: https://www.nps.gov/thingstodo/wind-cave-natural-entrance.htm
  - Natural entrance "only about ten inches wide"; wind audible rushing through it.
- Exploration Pushes Wind Cave to 5th Longest in the World (dated January 2005): https://www.nps.gov/wica/learn/news/exploration-pushes-wind-cave-to-5th-longest-in-the-world.htm
  - "The official length of the cave is now 114 miles long" as of January 8, 2005, when explorers mapped 2,715 feet of new passage, making it the fifth-longest cave in the world at that time.
- WebSearch summary re: black-footed ferret: after a thirty-year absence, black-footed ferrets returned to Wind Cave. October 2019 release of 29 ferrets (raised at the U.S. Fish & Wildlife Service National Black-footed Ferret Conservation Center, Colorado) added to an estimated existing population of 18-20 animals. An earlier release referenced seven animals released July 4 from Conata Basin stock.

Conflicting/evolving figures: cave length has been reported at different points as 114 miles (2005, 5th longest), 119.6 miles (a 2006 WebSearch summary, 4th longest), 129.8 miles (Geologic Resources Inventory Report, via WebSearch summary), and 168.02 miles/6th longest (a 2025 WebSearch summary not confirmed by direct nps.gov fetch). No dedicated current statistics page was found with one settled figure. The essay states the directly-sourced 2005/114-mile milestone explicitly with its date, and notes the ranking has changed multiple times as exploration continues, rather than asserting one current "true" length or rank.
