# Mammoth Cave sources

Live pages used:

- Park statistics: https://www.nps.gov/maca/learn/management/statistics.htm
  - Total park area 52,830 acres. Lowest surface point 411 feet (Green River near Brownsville); highest surface point 852 feet (Brooks Knob). Total surveyed cave length 426 miles. Lowest cave elevation 412 feet (Echo River); highest 810 feet (top of elevator shaft); total cave relief 398 feet. Congressional authorization May 25, 1926; established July 1, 1941. World's longest cave system designation September 9, 1972. UNESCO World Heritage Site October 27, 1981. International Dark Sky Park October 28, 2021.
- Timeline: https://www.nps.gov/maca/learn/historyculture/timeline.htm
  - Native Americans explored and mined the cave 5,000-2,000 BCE. Saltpeter mining began around 1792, ceased in 1815. Park established July 1, 1941, with 45,310 acres at that time. UNESCO World Heritage Site 1981. Accepted as the core of an International Biosphere Reserve in 1990.
- Stephen Bishop biography: https://www.nps.gov/people/stephen-bishop.htm
  - Arrived at the cave in 1838, enslaved, placed as a guide by owner Franklin Gorin. Crossed the Bottomless Pit, opening new passage; discovered Mammoth Dome and the Ruins of Karnak. Drew a map from memory in 1842, later published in Rambles in the Mammoth Cave During the Year 1844. Gained freedom, later a paid guide. Died summer 1857; buried in the Old Guide's Cemetery.
- Search of nps.gov results on eyeless fish: two eyeless fish species (Amblyopsis spelaea, Typhlichthys subterraneus) and the endangered Kentucky cave shrimp live in the cave. Amblyopsis spelaea was the first known eyeless cavefish, discovered 1842.

Conflicting figures: the timeline's 45,310-acre figure is the acreage at the park's 1941 founding; the statistics page's 52,830 acres is the current total. These are different points in time, not a genuine conflict (comparable to Grand Teton's 1929/1950 figures already noted in the build-out log), so both are kept with their dates stated.
