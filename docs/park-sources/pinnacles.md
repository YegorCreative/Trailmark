# Pinnacles sources

Live pages used:

- Faults: https://www.nps.gov/pinn/learn/nature/faults.htm
  - Pinnacles Rocks believed to originate from the Neenach Volcano near present-day Lancaster, California. Distinctive breccias at Pinnacles seen elsewhere only in the Neenach Formation, about 195 miles (314 km) southeast. San Andreas Fault right-lateral movement about 0.59 inches (1.5 cm) per year carried the rock north. Volcanic activity (rhyolitic magma and flows) about 23 million years ago, Miocene epoch. Chalone Creek Fault, still active, believed to mark the San Andreas's former position from that era. San Andreas Fault now about 4 miles (6 km) east of the park.
- Geologic formations: https://www.nps.gov/pinn/learn/nature/geologicformations.htm
  - Confirms "approximately 23 million years ago, volcanic activity extruded this formation"; names breccia, rhyolite, and fanglomerates as the park's rock types.
- WebSearch summary of nps.gov results: acreage approximately 10,799 hectares (26,686 acres). Talus caves named as a rare feature; Bear Gulch and Balconies Caves named specifically.
- WebSearch summary of nps.gov results re: establishment: national monument January 16, 1908 (President Theodore Roosevelt); national park January 10, 2013.
- WebSearch summary of nps.gov results re: condors: release site since 2003, partnership with U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service and Ventana Wildlife Society, goal of releasing 20-30 condors over 15 years; wild condor population reached 276 by 2017. Over 200 bird species documented in the park since 1908.
- Condors at Pinnacles: https://www.nps.gov/pinn/learn/nature/condor.htm
  - Confirms the 2003 program start year and names lead ammunition as a current threat to condors and other wildlife.

No page opened for this essay gives a specific wingspan or current population figure for the park's own condor cohort, so none is stated as a precise number.
