# Hot Springs sources

Live pages used:

- History and culture: https://www.nps.gov/hosp/learn/historyculture/index.htm
  - Andrew Jackson, April 20, 1832. Called the oldest current national park unit, forty years before Yellowstone. National park March 4, 1921, the 18th. Lamar Bathhouse 1923. Government free bathhouse 1921. Stephen Mather.
- Brochure: https://www.nps.gov/hosp/planyourvisit/park-brochure-continued.htm and https://www.nps.gov/hosp/planyourvisit/brochures.htm
  - Dunbar and Hunter, 1804. First reservation to protect a natural resource. 5,500 forested acres. African American bathhouses from 1905. Park name 1921.
- Centennial news: https://www.nps.gov/hosp/learn/news/celebrate100yearsofhotsprings.htm
  - March 4, 1921. 47 springs. Water about 4,400 years old. Only federally controlled hot springs managed for public health and use.
- Experience the water: https://www.nps.gov/hosp/planyourvisit/thermal-springs.htm
  - About 4,400-year-old water. Geology begins with rocks more than 400 million years old.
- Plan your visit: https://www.nps.gov/hosp/planyourvisit/index.htm
  - No outdoor soaking. Baths in bathhouses. 26 miles of trail on the homepage.
- People: https://www.nps.gov/hosp/learn/historyculture/people.htm
  - Thousands of years of novaculite and spring use.

Fees, campground rates, and which bathhouse is open are left to NPS. The 1832 four sections and the modern 5,500 acres are different measurements.
