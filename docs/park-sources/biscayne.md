# Biscayne sources

Live pages opened:

- The Birth of Biscayne National Park, last updated February 2, 2017: https://www.nps.gov/bisc/learn/historyculture/the-birth-of-biscayne-national-park.htm
  - President Lyndon B. Johnson signed the monument bill on October 18, 1968.
  - Spite highway on Elliott Key: six lanes wide, seven miles long. Later the park's only hiking trail.
  - Enlargements and a name change are mentioned. The day of the name change is not on this page.
  - Nearly 50 keys north of Key Largo. Jetport plan of 8,000 acres. Seadade channel of 40 feet, announced 1962. City of Islandia in 1961.
  - Greater Miami: 4 million residents and 10 million annual visitors, as stated on that page.
- Enabling legislation, last updated February 8, 2017: https://www.nps.gov/bisc/learn/management/enabling-legislation.htm
  - Quoted U.S. Code establishes Biscayne National Park, abolishes the October 18, 1968 monument, and requires a boundary description not more than one year after June 28, 1980.
  - No entrance or admission fee, notwithstanding any other provision of law. Fishing follows Florida law unless limited for conservation.
- History and culture, last updated June 18, 2025: https://www.nps.gov/bisc/learn/historyculture/index.htm
  - 10,000 years. Boca Chita lighthouse by Mark Honeywell in the late 1930s. Lancelot Jones. Shell middens. Dozens of shipwrecks.
- Nature, last updated June 10, 2021: https://www.nps.gov/bisc/learn/nature/index.htm
  - Four ecosystems and ecotones. Manatees, sea turtles, hundreds of birds and fish, plants and insects found nowhere else in the United States. Urban pressure.

The park year in the essay is the June 28, 1980 date named in the statute on the enabling page. A separate "signed on" sentence was not copied from the foundation PDF. The turtle drawing is not a count.
