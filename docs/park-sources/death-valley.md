## 2026-09-27 fact check

- **Place name — "Devils Hole"**: the dedicated NPS page (https://www.nps.gov/places/devils-hole.htm) consistently spells it "Devils Hole," no apostrophe (matches the USGS Board on Geographic Names convention used elsewhere on this site, e.g. "Devils Garden" at Arches). A different NPS page (deva/learn/nature/index.htm) uses "Devil's Hole" inconsistently in prose, but the place's own dedicated page is the authority. Fixed all 6 occurrences in js/park-content.js.
- **"Hottest place" attribution**: the overview lead previously read "...NPS calls the basin the hottest place in the world," incorrectly attaching the heat record to Badwater Basin. The source page (deva/learn/nature/index.htm) actually says "...the driest and lowest spot in North America, and the hottest in the world" as a general description of the park, not the basin specifically. An NPS news release (https://www.nps.gov/deva/learn/news/record-heat-at-death-valley.htm) has the park superintendent calling the park "the hottest place on Earth," and ties the specific record temperature reading (134°F, July 10, 1913 — the highest reliably recorded on Earth) to Furnace Creek, not Badwater Basin. Reworded the overview lead and the Summer season line to attribute the record to Death Valley / Furnace Creek, not the basin.
