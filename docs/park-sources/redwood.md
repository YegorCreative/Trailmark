# Redwood sources

Live pages used:

- Basic information: https://www.nps.gov/redw/planyourvisit/basicinfo.htm
  - Joint management agreement between California Department of Parks and Recreation and NPS in 1994, bringing all four parks (Redwood National Park plus Jedediah Smith, Del Norte Coast, and Prairie Creek Redwoods state parks) under joint management. Redwood National Park established 1968; the three state parks established by California in the 1920s. 37 miles of Pacific coastline named on this page (a separate WebSearch summary cites "nearly 56 km (35 mi)"; the essay uses 35 miles from the more specific Geodiversity-style figure). UN World Heritage Site designation 1980.
- Plants: https://www.nps.gov/redw/learn/nature/plants.htm
  - In protected valleys and alluvial flats, coast redwoods "frequently exceed 300 feet." On drier ridges such as Lady Bird Johnson Grove, they average 200 feet or less due to water stress and wind damage. Beach, dune, and scrub communities buffer redwoods from salt spray and wind.
- Should I Hike to Hyperion?: https://www.nps.gov/redw/planyourvisit/hyperion.htm
  - Hyperion discovered 2006. Height "cannot be observed from the ground" per NPS; no figure given on this page. Tom McDonald Creek area closed because it "contains sensitive and unique park resources that require limited human exposure." "Hiking within this closure could result in a fine or jail." "Forests grow by the inch and die by the foot."
- WebSearch summary of nps.gov and related results: total acreage 138,999 acres (56,251 hectares). Established October 2, 1968, same act as North Cascades. About 43,000 acres of old-growth redwood forest are among the last remnants of a historic band up to 25 miles wide that once ran from Monterey, California to southern Oregon. A major congressional expansion about ten years after 1968 added roughly 48,000 acres.

Hyperion's current measured height is not stated as a confirmed figure on the NPS page opened for this essay (a 2026 measurement of 381.3 feet circulated in search results but is not sourced to nps.gov directly); no specific height figure for Hyperion is given in the essay.
