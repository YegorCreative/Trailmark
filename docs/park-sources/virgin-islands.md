# Virgin Islands sources

Live pages used:

- WebSearch summary of NPS results: authorized August 2, 1956. Acreage approximately 14,948 total: 7,259 acres terrestrial/shoreline, 5,650 acres adjacent submerged lands added 1962, 128 acres on Hassel Island (Charlotte Amalie Harbor, St. Thomas) added 1978. Park covers more than half of St. John. Preserves coral reefs, seagrass beds, coves, beaches, mangrove forests, salt ponds, subtropical forest, and the largest and most intact dry tropical forest remaining in the Caribbean.
- St. John History Timeline: https://www.nps.gov/viis/learn/timeline.htm
  - Laurance Rockefeller sailed to St. John in 1952 and soon after donated land to establish the park; also provided electricity infrastructure. Taino cultural presence AD 1200-1500 noted generally. In 1998, excavation began on a Taino temple at Cinnamon Bay.
- WebSearch summary re: Annaberg: Annaberg Plantation owned in the 1720s-1730s by Frederick Moth, first Danish governor of St. Croix; became St. John's largest sugar-producing estate by the early 1800s, also producing molasses and rum for export; more than 600 enslaved people lived and worked there at peak production. Remains include windmill tower, factory, and slave quarters; docents available seasonally November-April.
- WebSearch summary re: Reef Bay petroglyphs: Taino people carved prehistoric petroglyphs on basalt outcrops in the remote Reef Bay Valley; some carvings sit above a spring-fed reflection pool and are thought to represent water. The Reef Bay Guided Hike is just under 3 miles one way, strenuous, over uneven rocky terrain.
- WebSearch summary re: Trunk Bay: underwater snorkel trail along the western side of Trunk Cay with informational plaques; parrotfish, yellowtail snapper, and angelfish named; 30-60 minutes; $5 entry fee; reef-safe sunscreen and standing only in sand advised.
