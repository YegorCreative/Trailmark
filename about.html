About TrailMark

An illustrated record of the 63 U.S. national parks, published one park at a time.

What TrailMark is

TrailMark is an illustrated archive of all 63 U.S. national parks. It is not a trip planner and it is not a government site. Each park is written and drawn as its own chapter, then added to the archive when that chapter is ready.

The Parks index lists every park. Open essays can be read. The rest are marked Coming soon until their pages are published.

How a park page is made

Research starts with the National Park Service. The pages used for a park are written down in a source note for that park. Establishment years and numbers come from those pages. If a figure was not on a page that was opened, it is left out.

The writing is specific to that place. It does not use filler, and it does not treat fees, permits, shuttles, or road closures as permanent facts. For anything that changes, the page points to the Park Service.

The art for each park is a set of illustrations created for TrailMark: a header painting, a circular badge, and an extra piece, usually an animal or another living detail. The paintings are not photographs.

The badge collection

Every park has one mark. The marks are circular illustrations, one for each of the 63 parks, and the homepage holds them as a wall. A mark becomes a link when that park’s essay is published. Until then it stays on the wall as a record of what is still to come.

Who’s building it

TrailMark is designed and built by Yegor Hambaryan, a web designer and developer based in New York. He studies Photographic Science at RIT, and he designs websites for businesses and ships iOS apps to the App Store.

That photographic training shows up here: TrailMark pays attention to light, landscape, and how a place is actually seen.

More of his work: yegorcreative.com

The repository is on GitHub at github.com/YegorCreative.

Accuracy and sources

The National Park Service is the authority for facts about the parks. TrailMark records the pages it used and would rather omit a number than guess one. If a page is wrong, report it through Contact.

Accessibility

TrailMark aims for WCAG 2.2 AA. Pages work from the keyboard, including the menu, the park filters, and the chapter links on a park page. Reduced motion turns off the homepage crossfade, parallax, badge tilt, and scroll reveals, and leaves the content visible. Text is kept at a contrast of at least 4.5:1, including type that sits over paintings.

If something on the site blocks you, say so on the Contact page.
