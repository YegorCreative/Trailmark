The words belong to that place. A second park gets a second chapter, written again from the ground up.

Made by Yegor Hambaryan

I’m Yegor, a web designer and developer based in New York. I study Photographic Science at RIT, design websites for businesses, and ship iOS apps to the App Store.

TrailMark is where those things meet: light, landscape, and a website built to be clear, fast, and easy to explore.

Why I’m building this

I believe the national parks are some of the clearest pictures of God’s creation we get to walk through. Standing in some of these places has taken my breath away: the scale, the light, the quiet. TrailMark is my way of appreciating that creation, one park at a time, and inviting others to see it too.

Clear, fast, and obvious about what to do next.

How the facts stay right

  1. 01

    The Park Service comes first. Every chapter is researched from National Park Service pages, and the sources are recorded for each park.

  2. 02

    No guessed numbers. If a figure can’t be confirmed, it’s left out.

  3. 03

    What changes points to NPS. Fees, permits, shuttles, and closures link to the Park Service instead of being written in stone.

Spot a mistake? Tell me on the Contact page.

Accessibility

TrailMark is built to WCAG 2.2 AA: keyboard-friendly, readable contrast, and reduced-motion support. Something not working for you? Let me know.

Share your park photos

Been to one of these parks? Send me your photos. I review every one, and the ones that fit get added to that park's page with your name on them.

You keep the rights to your photo. Sending it gives TrailMark permission to publish it with credit. Location data is removed before anything is posted.
